# March 2026 – Governance, Risk, and Compliance (GRC) Sprint

## Overview

In March 2026, I participated in a Governance, Risk, and Compliance (GRC) sprint focused on understanding how organisations manage cybersecurity risk, enforce security policies, and ensure regulatory compliance.

This phase introduced a broader perspective on cybersecurity, shifting from purely technical analysis to risk management, policy development, and organisational security governance.

The objective was to understand how security decisions are made at a strategic level and how GRC frameworks support overall security operations.

---

## Key Responsibilities

During this sprint, I contributed to activities focused on identifying, assessing, and managing security risks within a simulated organisational environment.

My responsibilities included:

- Identifying potential security risks across systems and processes  
- Analysing the likelihood and impact of different threats  
- Supporting the development of risk registers  
- Reviewing and contributing to security policies  
- Documenting findings in alignment with compliance requirements  

This experience helped me understand how organisations translate technical risks into business-level decisions.

---

## GRC Activities

### Risk Identification & Assessment

I worked on identifying risks that could affect organisational systems and operations.

This involved:

- analysing potential threats and vulnerabilities  
- assessing likelihood and impact  
- categorising risks based on severity  

Key learning:

Not all risks are equal — prioritisation is essential to effective security management.

---

### Risk Register Development

I contributed to documenting risks in a structured format using a risk register.

This included:

- defining risk descriptions  
- assigning risk levels  
- recommending mitigation strategies  

Key learning:

A well-structured risk register improves visibility and supports informed decision-making.

---

### Security Policies & Governance

As part of the sprint, I contributed to the development and review of key organisational security policies.

Policies created and refined included:

- **Access Control Policy** – defining how user access is granted, managed, and reviewed based on least privilege principles  
- **Password Policy** – establishing requirements for strong authentication, including complexity and rotation  
- **Incident Response Policy** – outlining procedures for detecting, reporting, and responding to security incidents  
- **Acceptable Use Policy (AUP)** – defining appropriate use of organisational systems and resources  
- **Data Protection Policy** – ensuring sensitive data is handled, stored, and protected securely  

Key learning:

Policies provide a structured foundation for enforcing security controls and ensuring consistent behaviour across an organisation.

---

### Compliance and Regulatory Awareness

I was introduced to the importance of compliance within cybersecurity.

This included understanding:

- organisational obligations to meet regulatory standards  
- the role of compliance in reducing legal and operational risk  
- how security frameworks guide best practices  

Key learning:

Compliance supports accountability and ensures organisations maintain a strong security posture.

---

### Risk Mitigation Strategies

I analysed how organisations respond to identified risks.

Common approaches included:

- risk mitigation (reducing the likelihood or impact)  
- risk transfer (e.g., insurance or third-party services)  
- risk acceptance (for low-impact risks)  
- risk avoidance (eliminating the activity entirely)  

Key learning:

Risk management is about making informed decisions rather than eliminating all risk.

---

## Key Security Insights

Through this sprint, I developed a broader understanding of cybersecurity beyond technical operations:

- Security must align with business objectives  
- Risk-based decision-making is central to cybersecurity  
- Governance ensures consistency in security practices  
- Policies act as the foundation for enforcing controls  
- Technical vulnerabilities must be translated into business risk

- thinking in terms of business impact

This experience helped me develop a more well-rounded approach to cybersecurity.
